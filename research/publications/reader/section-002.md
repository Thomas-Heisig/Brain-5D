# Abstract

This scientific treatise integrates the epistemic genealogy of artificial intelligence, the technical architecture of a plastic spiking system, and the practical conditions of human control. Borrowed intelligence denotes a relation of epistemic descent rather than a denial of novelty. Recursive technogenesis denotes machine participation in the construction and selection of subsequent technical systems. The analysis separates functional agency, consciousness, moral status, normative autonomy, and infrastructural independence.

Brain-5D is treated as a bounded research platform, not as evidence for general artificial intelligence. Its five-dimensional neural address space is an architectural hypothesis and is distinct from the five-axis philosophical description of intelligence. The empirical synthesis examines documented recurrence, stability, interference-screening, diagnostic-suite, storage, and restore results. Reported effects are retained together with their limitations: dirty-source provenance, pending review, a semantic-status conflict, unresolved independence of initialization, and restricted task scope. Technical test success, descriptive experimental data, accepted registry evidence, and philosophical interpretation remain separate categories.

The proposed research program combines causal component isolation, preregistered comparisons, held-out evaluation, nonanthropomorphic embodiment, modality-specific gateways, resource-aware adaptation, and provenance-preserving data compaction. Its broader argument is that increasing delegated competence need not remove human responsibility, but it raises the requirements for meaningful review, intervention, reproducibility, and institutional accountability.
