# Abstract

This treatise investigates how capabilities of hybrid artificial systems can be attributed to their causal carriers and subjected to verifiable control. It distinguishes literature-based findings, conditional formal arguments, project-specific secondary analysis, and empirical hypotheses awaiting evaluation. Brain-5D is a research platform and project name, not a demonstrated claim that five-dimensional architectures outperform alternatives.

Revision 1.1 adds three explicit methodological results. First, injective relabelling of a fixed network preserves its decoded trajectory when state-transition rules do not depend on numerical coordinates and initial states, inputs, parameters, and random sequences are appropriately coupled. This conditional result does not apply when geometry changes connectivity, delays, or learning. Second, constructed examples establish that population activity summaries do not identify retained information. Third, observationally identical responses can be generated by a neural core or an external information path, making intervention necessary for the proposed attribution claims. An executable standard-library program checks finite witnesses through 28 deterministic checks. It is not a Brain-5D learning experiment or an independent empirical replication.

Existing recurrence, restore, and storage reports retain their original provenance and restricted interpretation. A reported increase from three to thirty-three spikes is not treated as evidence of memory. Research-question status, evidence-record status, technical execution, and human review are kept separate. The revision does not promote or alter canonical evidence records.

The literature method distinguishes planned database searches, actual search routes, access to particular source sections, and argumentative use. The inherited bibliography is not represented as a fully read or systematically reviewed corpus. An extended glossary supplies definitions, neighbouring concepts, examples, counterexamples, operational indicators, and limits. Draft protocols specify dimensionality controls, held-out learning and recall tasks, intervention-based attribution, and control audits. Their prospective status is explicit. The contribution is a more testable account of what the system's observations can and cannot establish; architectural superiority and robust cognitive capabilities remain unconfirmed.
